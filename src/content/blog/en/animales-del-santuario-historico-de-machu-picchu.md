When people think of Machu Picchu, the first image is usually stone terraces and mist-covered mountains. But the place is also a wildlife sanctuary, and those who look up from the stone paths discover that **the animals of Machu Picchu** are an essential part of the experience: from llamas grazing calmly between Inca walls to brightly colored birds living in the cloud forest.

The short answer: in the Historic Sanctuary of Machu Picchu you can find **llamas** (almost certainly), **hummingbirds, butterflies and many birds** (with a good chance if you know where to look) and, with a lot of luck and patience, emblematic species such as the **Andean cock-of-the-rock** or the **Andean condor**. Animals like the **spectacled bear**, the **puma** or the **deer** do live here, but they are so elusive that seeing one is the exception, not the rule.

In this **Peru Beyond** guide we explain which species exist, where and at what time you have the best chances, the indicative likelihood of spotting each one and, above all, how to watch them without causing harm.

---

## 1. A sanctuary where the Andes meet the Amazon

The Historic Sanctuary of Machupicchu is a protected natural area managed by SERNANP, and it is also a UNESCO World Heritage Site for both its cultural and natural value. Its animal richness comes from geography: within a few kilometers the terrain changes from high, cold slopes to humid cloud forests close to the edge of the Amazon. That change in altitude creates many different habitats, and each one has its own residents.

About species counts: depending on the source and the year consulted, the numbers of birds and mammals in the sanctuary vary. For that reason, in this article **we do not repeat a single figure**; we prefer to tell you what is documented: it is an area of high diversity that brings together Andean and Amazonian ecosystems and shelters threatened species, according to the sanctuary's own master plan and SERNANP's monitoring studies.

## 2. The llamas: the citadel's most famous neighbors

It is hard to visit the citadel without running into them. Llamas (*Lama glama*) walk and rest on the terraces, and are usually the first thing travelers photograph alongside the classic view. They are **domestic**, not wild, animals and live on the site as part of the sanctuary's landscape; it is commonly explained that they help keep the terrace grass short.

**Where to see them:** on the agricultural terraces and open plazas of the citadel, especially in the upper area near the classic viewpoint and in the wide grassy sectors. Llamas move around the whole route, so you are likely to meet them without looking.

### 2.1. How to behave around llamas

- **Do not touch or pet them**, even if they seem docile.
- **Do not give them food.** Feeding animals, domestic or wild, is among the prohibited acts in the citadel.
- **Keep a sensible distance**, and never get between a mother and her calf.
- **Do not stand behind them**: they can kick if startled.
- **Always follow the marked circuit** and never step into restricted areas to get closer for a photo.

> **💡 Peru Beyond Tip:** The best llama photos are taken without touching them. Arrive early, stay on the path and wait: with patience, they will place themselves in a great frame with the terraces behind. Use your zoom instead of walking closer.

## 4. Andean cock-of-the-rock: Peru's national bird

It is probably the **star of the sanctuary's birdlife**. *Rupicola peruviana* is a cloud forest bird, famous for the male's bright plumage and for its displays: males gather at traditional sites to court females with hops, wing flaps and loud calls.

**How to improve your chances:**

1. Hire a **bird guide** who knows the gathering sites and recognizes the calls.
2. Go out **early**, in the soft dawn light.
3. Walk quietly and slowly; noise scares birds away.
4. Bring **binoculars** (8x42 is a widely used configuration for birdwatching) and a camera with zoom.

It is not a bird you will see from the citadel's main circuit; you look for it in forested areas. If you do not see it, it does not mean you visited badly: it is simply a bird that shows itself when it wants to.

## 5. The Andean or spectacled bear: the great absentee (almost always)

The **spectacled bear** (*Tremarctos ornatus*) is South America's only bear and a symbol of Andean wildlife. It gets its name from the pale rings around its eyes. It lives in the sanctuary, and camera-trap monitoring has recorded it, but it is a shy animal that avoids humans and moves through remote forests.

**Realistic likelihood:** very low. Almost all visitors, even those who spend days in the area, never see one. Treat it as an extraordinary bonus, never as the goal of your trip. And if you do see one by chance, keep your distance, do not make loud noises and do not try to approach.

## 6. Andean condor: the glider of the Andes

The **Andean condor** (*Vultur gryphus*) is one of the largest flying birds in the world and an Andean symbol. Around Machu Picchu it is not as frequent as in famous canyons such as Colca, but it **can appear** gliding over high areas, using thermal air currents, especially on clear days.

**Tip:** look up toward the ridges and rock walls. It is recognized by its huge wingspan, wings with "fingers" at the tips and, in the adult male, the fleshy comb and white collar. Sightings are a stroke of luck; never guarantee them to anyone.

## 7. Hummingbirds, butterflies and orchids: the small life of the cloud forest

The real stars of the cloud forest are small.

- **Hummingbirds:** several species visit flowers on the sanctuary's slopes and in Machupicchu town. They are easier to see once the sun is out and shrubs are in bloom. Watch a shrub with tubular flowers calmly for several minutes: sooner or later they arrive.
- **Butterflies:** the sanctuary is famous among naturalists for its variety of butterflies, which appear on sunny, humid trails, especially in the lower, warmer areas.
- **Orchids (context):** the sanctuary is known for its many orchid species, part of the same ecosystem that feeds insects and hummingbirds. Remember that **plants must never be cut or removed**.

> **💡 Peru Beyond Tip:** If nature is your interest, dedicate a morning to walking forest trails instead of going straight to the citadel. Most small-bird sightings happen at the forest edges, not on the main stone circuit.

---

## 8. Viscachas, deer, taruca and pumas

- **Viscacha** (*Lagidium peruanum*): a rodent that looks like a long-tailed rabbit, living among rocks and stone walls. It often appears sunning itself early or as the afternoon fades.
- **Deer:** SERNANP has recorded it on camera in the sanctuary. It is shy and seen mostly in the first hours of the day in quiet areas.
- **Taruca** (*Hippocamelus antisensis*): an Andean deer of high, rocky terrain. It is typical of higher altitudes, so it is not a realistic expectation for the citadel circuit.
- **Puma** (*Puma concolor*): SERNANP reports it on camera traps; it is a solitary, nocturnal, very discreet cat. Visitor sightings are exceptional; it is not a risk on the normal tourist circuit.

## 11. Seasons: when to go to see wildlife

| Season | Months (approx.) | Wildlife advantages | Keep in mind |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Dry** | May to September | Clearer skies, better visibility and trails in better shape | More visitors, very cold mornings |
| **Rainy** | November to March | Very green, active forest, flowering, lots of insect life | Mist, rain and slippery trails |
| **Transition** | April and October | Balance between weather and fewer crowds | Variable weather |

Remember that the Inca Trail usually closes for maintenance in February and that access may be suspended during heavy rain; always confirm with official sources.

## 12. What NOT to do: responsible wildlife watching

The sanctuary's wildlife is part of the protected heritage. These rules protect the animals and also you:

- **Do not feed or touch the llamas or any animal.** It is among the prohibited acts and breaking the rule can mean expulsion without a refund.
- **Do not enter with pets**; only guide dogs are allowed.
- **Do not leave trash**: waste attracts animals and harms the ecosystem.
- **Do not use drones** or play loud music: they disturb birds.
- **Do not use flash** on animals, and do not chase them for a photo.
- **Do not cut or collect plants, flowers or orchids.**
- **Keep your distance from any wild mammal**, and do not chase or call it.
- **Follow the instructions of park rangers and your guide.**

## 13. Quick birdwatching guide

1. **Gear:** binoculars (8x42 is a versatile option), a camera with zoom, layered clothing and a rain jacket.
2. **Guide:** hire a local bird guide; their eyes and ears are worth more than any equipment.
3. **Timing:** dawn and the last hours of the afternoon.
4. **Attitude:** silence, slow movements, patience. A good vantage point beats walking a lot.
5. **Log:** carry a notebook or a bird app to record what you see.
6. **Health:** above 2,000 m, acclimatize well before heading out on walks.

## 14. How to include wildlife in your Machu Picchu trip

The most practical approach is to spend **at least one extra day** in Machupicchu town to walk forest trails and see the citadel without rushing. A single-day tour covers the classic circuit but leaves little room for slow observation. If nature is your thing, a 2 or 3 day itinerary gives you better odds.

**Checklist:**

- [ ] Book your Machu Picchu entry and train in advance.
- [ ] Ask for a bird guide if that is your interest.
- [ ] Bring binoculars, a camera and layered clothing.
- [ ] Remember the rules: do not touch or feed animals.
- [ ] Confirm current schedules and rules on official sources.

## 15. Conclusion

Machu Picchu is a place where history and nature live side by side. Llamas are almost guaranteed; hummingbirds and butterflies are a reward for those who look calmly; and the cock-of-the-rock, the condor and the bear are experiences that depend on patience, a good guide and a little luck. What matters is to travel with realistic expectations and watch with respect: the best sighting is the one that does not disturb the animal's life.

If you are searching for **Peru travel packages from Mexico**, the first thing you will notice is that two offers that look identical can be very different: one includes flights and the other doesn't, one books your Machu Picchu ticket and the other leaves that task to you, one names your hotel and the other only promises a "tourist category". The gap between a smooth trip and a logistical headache is almost never in the price, but in what is written (or not written) in the quote.

The short answer: **a good Peru package from Mexico should tell you clearly what is included, what is not, which Machu Picchu date and circuit you get, what happens if you cancel and who is responsible if something goes wrong**. This guide gives you a method to review that, compare offers and ask the right questions, written for travelers in Mexico who are weighing several agencies.

## 1. Types of Peru Travel Packages from Mexico: Comparison Table

Before comparing prices, identify **which type of package** you are comparing. A package with flights is not the same as a land-only one, and a fixed circuit is not the same as a tailor-made trip.

| Package type | What it usually includes | What is usually left out | Best for | Risk to watch |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **All-inclusive with flights from Mexico** | Mexico City–Lima flight, hotels, transfers, tours, sometimes train | Meals not listed, tips, extras | Travelers who want one payment and one responsible party | Flights with long layovers or checked bags not included |
| **Land package (no international flight)** | Hotels, transfers, guided tours, train and bus to Machu Picchu | Mexico City–Lima flight, domestic flights, insurance | Those who want to choose their own airline, miles or dates | Poor coordination between flight arrival and first service |
| **Cusco and Machu Picchu only (3 to 5 days)** | Cusco, Sacred Valley, Machu Picchu, train | Lima, southern coast, Lima–Cusco flight | Short getaways or a second trip | Little time to acclimatize to altitude |
| **Lima + South circuit (6 to 10 days)** | Lima, Paracas, Huacachina, Cusco, Machu Picchu | Domestic flight, some tickets, meals | First trip to Peru | Very tight pace if the itinerary has no breathing room |
| **Tailor-made** | Whatever you agree on: hotels, pace, experiences | Depends on the quote | Couples, families, anniversary trips | Scattered quotes that are hard to compare |
| **Adventure and trekking** | Guides, gear, camping or high-altitude routes | Prior acclimatization, altitude insurance | Active travelers | Underestimating altitude and fitness |

> **💡 Peru Beyond Tip:** Ask each agency to send its quote with **the same structure**: exact dates, number of nights per city, which Machu Picchu circuit, what type of train and which flights are included. Without that baseline, comparing prices is comparing apples to oranges.

## 2. First Things First: Entry Requirements for Mexican Travelers

Until April 2024 Mexican citizens needed a visa to enter Peru as tourists; since then **Peru has lifted that requirement** for ordinary Mexican passports through a supreme decree published by the Peruvian government. According to the rule, a tourist stay can reach up to 183 calendar days, although the actual period the immigration officer grants on arrival may be shorter.

- **Valid passport:** essential. Several sources recommend at least six months of validity; check it before you buy, not after.
- **Exit ticket:** keep your return or onward itinerary handy, as the airline or immigration may ask for it.
- **Accommodation details:** carry printed or downloaded details of your first night's hotel and your agency's contact information.
- **Yellow fever:** only suggested if you plan to go deep into the jungle (for example Manu, Tambopata or Iquitos). For Lima, Cusco, the Sacred Valley and Machu Picchu it is usually not required; confirm with your doctor and the health authority.

If the agency says nothing about documents, that is not a bad sign in itself, but it is a chance to ask. A serious agency knows which papers you need and explains them without being asked.

## 3. What a Package Should Include (and What to Ask About Each Item)

A package is like a chain: if one link is unclear, the whole trip can get complicated. Review each item separately.

### 3.1. Mexico City – Lima Flights

There are nonstop flights between Mexico City and Lima operated by airlines such as Aeroméxico and LATAM; according to flight search engines, the trip takes around 6 hours, with small differences by airline and direction. Schedules change by season, so confirm them on the airline's official website.

- Ask whether the flight **is included** or whether you buy it yourself.
- If the agency includes it: is it **nonstop or with a layover**? Does it include **checked baggage**? What happens if the airline changes the schedule?
- If you buy it yourself: **do not buy the flight before your Machu Picchu and train dates are confirmed**, because those are the services with the most limited availability.

### 3.2. Domestic Flights (Lima–Cusco)

Many itineraries include a flight from Lima to Cusco, which avoids a very long overland journey. This is one of the items most often left "outside the package" without the traveler noticing. Check whether the domestic flight is included and whether the package accounts for the baggage limit, which on domestic flights is usually stricter than on international ones.

### 3.3. Hotels

"3-star tourist hotel" is not a hotel. Ask for the **hotel name in each city**, its category, location and whether breakfast is included. In Cusco the location matters because of the altitude and for walkability; in Machu Picchu Pueblo (Aguas Calientes), being close to the station and the bus is key if your ticket has an early entry time.

### 3.4. Train and Bus to Machu Picchu

The train is a central part of the trip to the citadel. There are several companies and service categories, and the difference between them shows in schedules, comfort and price. Ask which company, which service category and which departure time you are assigned, and whether the **bus up to the citadel** is included. If you want to compare services, read our guide to the types of trains to Machu Picchu.

### 3.5. Tickets and Guides

Machu Picchu tickets are a chapter of their own (see the next section). For the other sites (Sacsayhuamán, Pisac, Ollantaytambo, Moray, Maras), ask whether the package includes the tickets or whether you pay at the site. Sometimes the Cusco tourist ticket or the individual entrance fee is not included; the agency should tell you in writing.

Also check the **guide's language** (Spanish, English), whether the tour is **private or group** and the maximum group size.

### 3.6. Travel Insurance

It is almost never included. We recommend buying a policy with international medical coverage, altitude-related care and, if possible, trip cancellation or interruption. Check that it covers high-altitude activities if you plan to trek.

## 4. Machu Picchu: Limited Quota and Dated Tickets

This is where packages differ most. Citadel tickets are bought on the Ministry of Culture's official platform (Tu Boleto) and are subject to **a daily visitor cap**: for 2026 a general limit of 4,500 people per day was published, rising to 5,600 during high-season periods. The exact high-season dates and the rates can change from year to year, so check them at the official source.

The current system is organized into **3 circuits and 10 routes**, each with its own path, duration and entry time. Some circuits (the most requested) sell out weeks or months ahead.

So when reviewing a package, the question is not "does it include Machu Picchu?" but:

- **Which circuit and route** do I get?
- What **day and time** is my entry?
- Is the ticket **already purchased**, or bought "after you pay"?
- What happens if my preferred date **has no availability left**?
- Does my ticket match the **train schedule**?

> **💡 Peru Beyond Tip:** A serious package confirms your Machu Picchu circuit and date **before** asking for full payment, or explains clearly when it will. If the quota runs out, you need a written alternative (another date or a similar circuit), not a verbal promise.

Tickets are issued for a specific date and time and, according to the rules we have covered in our guides, cannot be changed easily once issued; that is why your travel date must be settled before buying. For more detail, read our guide to Machu Picchu tickets and circuits.

## 5. Red Flags When Buying Peru Packages from Mexico

Mexico's consumer protection agency (PROFECO) warns every holiday season about so-called "ghost agencies", which advertise prices that are too low and disappear after receiving payment. Even if your purchase is a package in Peru, the same common-sense rules apply.

  <div class="bp-compare-col"><h4>Red flags</h4><ul><li>Price far below the other quotes</li><li>Pressure to pay today for an "offer that expires"</li><li>Account in a person's name, no company</li><li>No contract or written terms</li><li>"Machu Picchu included" with no date or circuit</li><li>Evasive answers about cancellation</li></ul></div>

Some prevention steps you can take:

1. **Verify the agency.** Look up its company name, website, address and social media presence. If it is a Peruvian agency, check its registration with Peru's tourism authorities; if it is Mexican, you can consult PROFECO's Buró Comercial.
2. **Ask for the contract and read all of it.** PROFECO recommends receiving a contract listing the contracted services, the agency's stamp or letterhead and a proof of payment.
3. **Pay with a traceable method.** A credit card offers more options for disputes than a transfer to a personal account.
4. **Distrust urgency.** A real offer can survive 24 hours of comparison.
5. **Keep everything in writing.** Screenshots, emails and WhatsApp messages work as backup.

### 6.1. Budget: How to Think About It (Approximate)

We will not invent exact figures, because flight, train and hotel prices change by season, purchase date and availability. What we can give you is a way to organize your budget so that "outside the package" costs don't surprise you:

- **International flight Mexico City–Lima:** usually the most variable expense. Compare flexible dates and confirm included baggage.
- **Land package:** hotels, transfers, tours, train and bus. This is the item that compares best across agencies.
- **Domestic flight Lima–Cusco:** get a separate quote if it is not included.
- **Meals not included, tips, souvenirs and personal expenses.** As an internal Peru Beyond reference, only as an order of magnitude for budget-conscious travelers, our guides have used a range of 70 to 150 USD per person per day for in-destination spending; it is an **approximate** figure, not a rate, and depends on travel style.
- **Travel insurance:** usually a small part of the total, and appreciated only when it is used.

> **📌 Key Fact:** The best saving is not the lowest price but avoiding unexpected costs: a train date change, a ticket that wasn't included or an extra hotel night caused by a badly calculated flight.

## 7. Ideal Trip Length: 7, 10 or 14 Days

The question travelers from Mexico ask most is how many days they need. The answer depends on what you want to see and how much altitude affects you. Cusco sits at about 3,399 m, and it is wise to spend one or two days acclimatizing before the more demanding excursions.

| Length | What you can do | Pace | Best for |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **7 days** | Lima, Cusco, Sacred Valley and Machu Picchu | Intense, with little margin | A first trip with limited time |
| **10 days** | The above plus southern coast (Paracas, Ica) or a high-altitude excursion | Balanced | Most travelers |
| **14 days** | Full southern Andes, plus Puno, jungle or trekking | Relaxed | Those who want to see more without rushing |

## 8. Season: When to Travel and When to Book

The Peruvian Andes have a **dry season** (roughly May to October), with clearer skies and cold nights, and a **rainy season** (broadly November to March), with greener landscapes and a higher chance of rain. The dry season coincides with peak demand and with the higher daily cap at Machu Picchu, so availability runs out sooner.

From Mexico, also keep your own holiday periods in mind: Holy Week, summer break and year-end concentrate demand from Mexican travelers and push up flight and hotel prices. A simple rule:

- **June to August and holiday dates:** book several months ahead.
- **Rest of the year:** there is more room, but don't leave booking to the last minute, especially if you want a specific Machu Picchu circuit.
- **Rainy season:** travel is possible with good results, always with a flexible itinerary and suitable clothing.

For more detail, read our guide to the best time to travel to Peru.

## 9. Cancellation, Change and Refund Policies

This is what people read least and miss most when something unexpected happens. Before paying, clarify in writing:

- **How much is refunded if I cancel?** And up to what date.
- **Can I change the date?** At what cost and with how much notice.
- **Which services are non-refundable?** For example, Machu Picchu tickets already issued, trains bought in advance or airfares with restrictions.
- **What if the agency cancels?** Who returns the money and within what time.
- **What does travel insurance cover?** Cancellation due to illness, trip interruption, lost luggage or medical care.

> **💡 Peru Beyond Tip:** Keep the cancellation policy in the same email in which you accept the quote. If a dispute arises later, that message is your best evidence.

## 10. Payments and Invoicing

- **Currency:** confirm whether the price is in Mexican pesos or US dollars, and the exchange rate applied.
- **Deposit and balance:** ask what percentage is paid to reserve and when the balance is due.
- **Payment method:** prefer options with protection (card) and avoid transfers to personal accounts.
- **Receipt:** ask for a receipt in the company's name, detailing what was paid.
- **Invoice:** if you need an invoice, ask what type of receipt the agency issues. A Peruvian company issues receipts under its own country's rules, which may not be equivalent to a Mexican CFDI; if you need it for your accounting, confirm with the agency and your accountant before booking. This is a point we could not verify in general terms, because it depends on each traveler's case and each agency.

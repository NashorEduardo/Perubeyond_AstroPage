## 2. Planning Timeline: How Far in Advance to Book Your Trip

Because Peru enforces strict heritage conservation laws, waiting until the last minute often leads to sold-out attractions. Follow this recommended booking schedule:

### 2.1. 4 to 6 Months in Advance
- **Machu Picchu Entrance Tickets:** Peru's Ministry of Culture strictly limits daily visitor quotas. The most coveted route (**Circuit 2: Classic / Panoramic Circuit**) sells out months ahead, particularly between May and September.
- **Scenic Train Tickets:** Prime departure times on PeruRail and Inca Rail (such as morning Vistadome or 360° panoramic trains) should be secured simultaneously with citadel tickets.
- **Classic 4-Day Inca Trail:** If you plan to hike the original stone Incan highway, book **6 to 8 months ahead**, as only 500 total permits (including porters and guides) are released per day.

### 2.2. 2 to 3 Months in Advance
- **International and Domestic Flights:** Book flights arriving at Lima's Jorge Chávez International Airport (`LIM`) and domestic connections between Lima and Cusco (`CUZ`) with established carriers like LATAM.
- **Accommodations:** Secure boutique or brand hotels in safe tourist areas (Miraflores in Lima; Urubamba or Ollantaytambo in the Sacred Valley; Historic Center in Cusco).
- **Tour Packages & Private Guides:** Partner with an officially accredited agency like **Peru Beyond** to lock in insured tourist vehicles and vetted bilingual guides.

### 2.3. 1 Month to 2 Weeks in Advance
- **Travel Insurance:** Obtain comprehensive international travel insurance covering medical evacuation and high-altitude activities.
- **Footwear & Apparel:** Break in sturdy hiking shoes beforehand (never break in brand-new boots at Machu Picchu) and gather moisture-wicking layers and waterproof windbreakers.
- **US Dollars:** Secure crisp, unblemished dollar bills from your bank.

Entering Peru as an international tourist is remarkably straightforward:

1. **Valid Passport:** Must have at least 6 months of validity remaining from your arrival date and empty stamp pages.
2. **National ID Entry (South American Citizens):** Travelers holding citizenship in Argentina, Brazil, Bolivia, Colombia, Chile, Ecuador, Paraguay, or Uruguay can enter Peru using only their national ID card, exempt from passport requirements.
3. **Virtual Andean Migration Card (TAM Virtual):** At airport immigration desks, your entry is recorded digitally. Keep your entry stamp receipt, as authorized Peruvian hotels use it to waive the 18% domestic sales tax (IGV) for foreign tourists.
4. **Tourist Visa Exemption:** Citizens of the US, Canada, UK, EU, Australia, and New Zealand do not need a visa for tourism stays of up to 90 days.

The single most common mistake first-time travelers make is underestimating altitude. Flying directly from sea level into Cusco (**3,399 m / 11,152 ft**) and immediately climbing steep cobblestone streets often results in severe headaches, fatigue, and nausea (*soroche*).

**Peru Beyond's Stepped Acclimatization Strategy** resolves this by gradually introducing elevation:

```
Step 1: Sea Level (0 m)         ──► Lima / Paracas / Huacachina (1 - 2 days)
Step 2: Land in Cusco           ──► Direct private transfer down to Sacred Valley (2,800 m) (Day 3)
Step 3: Inca Citadel            ──► Machu Picchu (2,430 m / 7,972 ft) (Day 4)
Step 4: Imperial Capital        ──► Historic Cusco City & Nearby Ruins (3,399 m) (Day 5 - 6)
Step 5: High-Altitude Treks     ──► Humantay Lake (4,200 m) or Rainbow Mountain (5,200 m) (Day 7+)
```

### Why this route works:
- The **Sacred Valley of the Incas (Urubamba or Ollantaytambo)** sits at **2,800 meters (9,186 ft)**—nearly 600 meters lower than Cusco. The climate is warmer, air pressure is higher, and oxygen is noticeably richer. Sleeping your first two Andean nights in the valley eliminates 90% of altitude discomfort.
- **Machu Picchu is only 2,430 meters (7,972 ft)** above sea level in a lush subtropical cloud forest. Visiting from the Sacred Valley feels effortless.
- By the time you return to Cusco city (3,399 m), your body has naturally generated the extra red blood cells needed to explore vibrant plazas and ancient temples with energy.

> **💡 Peru Beyond Tip:** On your first 24 hours at high altitude, sip warm **muña** (Andean mint) or **coca tea**, drink plenty of bottled water, avoid heavy fatty meals, and skip alcohol.

### 6.1. Electricity and Plugs
- Standard voltage in Peru is **220V at 60Hz**.
- Wall outlets typically accommodate both standard North American two-prong plugs (Type A) and European round-pin plugs (Type C). Modern smartphones, cameras, and laptops handle 100-240V dual voltage automatically.

### 6.2. The Golden Packing Rule: Dressing in Layers
Andean weather features dramatic intraday temperature shifts:
- **Sunny daytime (10:00 AM to 3:00 PM):** 18°C to 22°C (64°F to 72°F) under intense high-altitude sunlight (wear breathable shirts, sunglasses, and a sunhat).
- **Crisp evenings (6:00 PM to 6:00 AM):** Temperatures plummet to 2°C to 6°C (35°F to 42°F), requiring thermal base layers, fleece, and light down jackets.
- **Essential gear:** SPF 50+ sunscreen, insect repellent (vital for Machu Picchu citadel and Aguas Calientes), broken-in trail shoes with rubber grip, and a reusable water bottle.

## 7. The 10 Most Common First-Timer Mistakes in Peru

Avoid these frequent pitfalls to guarantee a smooth experience:

1. **Assuming tickets can be bought at Machu Picchu's gate:** There are no ticket booths at the citadel entrance; every visitor must hold a timed, dated permit before arriving.
2. **Attempting 5,000-meter hikes on day one:** Booking Rainbow Mountain (5,200 m) the morning after landing in Cusco.
3. **Mismatched train and citadel entrance times:** Arriving in Aguas Calientes after your scheduled citadel entry slot (entrance tolerance is strictly 30 minutes).
4. **Dragging large rolling suitcases on the train:** Trains allow only 5 kg to 8 kg of hand luggage per traveler. Large luggage is stored safely for free at your hotel in Cusco or Ollantaytambo.
5. **Exchanging money with street changers:** Use verified indoor exchange houses.
6. **Drinking tap water:** Stick to sealed bottled water or hotel-filtered water.
7. **Skipping a certified guide at Machu Picchu:** Navigating the labyrinth of stone temples without an expert guide means missing 80% of its history and cosmological genius.
8. **Running out of local cash in small villages:** Assuming small street craft stalls take credit cards.
9. **Traveling without medical travel insurance:** Emergency high-altitude evacuation or private clinic care can be costly without coverage.
10. **Trying to see the entire country in 5 days:** Rushing through six cities leaves you exhausted. Prioritize quality and depth.

## 9. Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

### How many days do I need for my first trip to Peru?
We recommend 7 days as the ideal minimum for Lima, the Sacred Valley, Machu Picchu, and Cusco. With 10 to 12 days, you can add Paracas, Huacachina, and high-altitude treks.

### Do I need a visa to visit Peru?
Citizens of the US, Canada, EU, UK, Australia, and South America do not need a tourist visa for stays up to 90 days.

### Can South Americans travel to Peru with just a national ID?
Yes, citizens from Mercosur and Andean Community member countries can enter with their valid national ID card alone.

### What currency should I bring to Peru?
Bring clean, undamaged US Dollars to exchange into Peruvian Soles (PEN) at official exchange booths, while using credit cards for hotels and dining.

### Is it better to book an organized tour or travel independently?
Booking through an established local operator like Peru Beyond ensures guaranteed Machu Picchu permits, seamless train transfers, and personalized support without logistics stress.

### What is the best order of cities to avoid altitude sickness?
Start at sea level (Lima), transfer directly to the Sacred Valley (2,800 m), visit Machu Picchu (2,430 m), then explore Cusco city (3,399 m) and finish with mountain hikes.

### How far in advance should I book?
Book 4 to 6 months in advance, especially for travel between May and September, to secure classic Circuit 2 Machu Picchu tickets and panoramic trains.

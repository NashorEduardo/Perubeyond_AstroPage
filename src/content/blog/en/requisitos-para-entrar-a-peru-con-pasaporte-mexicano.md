If you are planning your trip and wondering about the **requirements to travel to Peru from Mexico**, here is the short answer: Mexicans holding an ordinary passport **do not need a visa** to enter as tourists, can stay **up to 183 days**, and only need to present a valid passport (with at least six months of validity), plus be ready to answer a few questions calmly at immigration. There is no form to fill out in advance and no consulate appointment to book.

Even so, on a long trip a few details are worth settling before you reach the airport: passport validity, your exit ticket, the virtual TAM, how much cash you can carry, which products you can bring in, vaccines if you plan to visit the jungle, and the rules for traveling with minors or pets. This **Peru Beyond** guide brings it all together, citing the official institutions (Migraciones Perú, SUNAT, the Ministry of Health, SENASA and Mexico's Ministry of Foreign Affairs) and flagging which details you should double-check before departure.

> **📌 Key Fact:** This guide reflects information available as of November 2026. Immigration rules can change on short notice (the visa exemption for Mexicans was suspended and then reinstated in April 2024). **Always verify with Migraciones Perú (gob.pe/migraciones), the SRE traveler's guide and your airline before you travel.**

## 2. Do Mexicans Need a Visa for Peru? What the Official Rule Says

**No visa is needed** for tourism with an ordinary Mexican passport. Recent history explains why the question causes doubt: in April 2024 Peru briefly suspended the visa exemption for Mexicans and, a few days later, reinstated it. **Supreme Decree No. 012-2024-RE**, published in the official gazette **El Peruano** on April 11, 2024, lifted that suspension and confirmed that Mexican nationals with a valid ordinary passport may enter for tourism or business without a visa, for a maximum stay of **183 calendar days**, continuous or in consecutive visits within one year.

The state news agency **Andina** reported the same measure that day. We have found no later rule reversing it, but precisely because of the 2024 precedent, our recommendation is that you **check Migraciones Perú's website or the Peruvian consulate in Mexico** before paying for your flights.

### 2.1. Conditions that do matter

- **Ordinary passport:** the exemption applies to this type of passport. If you travel with an official or diplomatic passport, check the specific regime.
- **Purpose of travel:** tourism or business. If you plan to study, work or live in Peru, you need a different immigration status and must apply for it.
- **Paid activities:** entering as a tourist does not authorize you to work. If you need to sign commercial or financial documents as a tourist, Migraciones says a free special permit can be requested through its Digital Agency.

> **💡 Peru Beyond Tip:** Save a screenshot of the decree (or the Migraciones notice) with its date on your phone. If an airline agent ever asks about a visa, you can show it calmly. Most of the time it is enough to confirm you are traveling on an ordinary Mexican passport.

## 3. Passport: Validity, Condition and Copies

The strictest requirement in practice is **passport validity**. Migraciones Perú advises foreign visitors to hold a passport valid for at least **six months**; without it you can be declared inadmissible and returned to your country of origin. Mexico's representation in Peru repeats the same warning. Airlines tend to apply this rule at check-in, so you are more likely to be denied boarding in Mexico City than in Lima.

### 3.1. Passport checklist

1. **Expiration:** count six months from the day you **arrive** in Peru and, for a long stay, through your departure date.
2. **Blank pages:** although Peru no longer stamps passports on international flights, you may need pages if you continue to other countries.
3. **Physical condition:** a damaged passport, with loose pages or a worn photo, may be rejected.
4. **Copies:** carry a printed copy and a photo in your email or cloud storage, kept apart from the original.
5. **Loss or theft:** Migraciones says to go to the nearest consulate to obtain a new document and a safe-conduct that allows you to return home. The SRE also recommends registering with **SIRME** (Mexico's registry of nationals abroad) to make consular assistance easier.

> **💡 Peru Beyond Tip:** If you have between six and eight months of validity left, renew before the trip. A passport problem is the hardest kind to solve while you are in Cusco or the jungle.

## 4. Virtual TAM, Forms and Immigration Control

One of the most common questions is whether you must fill out the **Andean Migration Card (TAM)** on paper or online before your flight. According to official information from **Migraciones Perú**, the current situation is this:

- Peru **eliminated the entry stamp** in passports at immigration control for international flights.
- The official record of your last migration movement is the **virtual TAM**, which can be downloaded **for free** from Migraciones' portal.
- The virtual TAM serves to **prove your authorized stay** if the National Police or Migraciones stop you in a verification operation.
- Its use at airport control posts is backed by a Migraciones resolution (No. 00119-2023-MIGRACIONES).

### 4.1. The new Jorge Chávez Airport

The **new terminal at Jorge Chávez International Airport** has operated since June 1, 2025 and has an automated immigration control system, according to Andina. In the first days, delays at immigration were reported and, later, Peruvian media said fewer than half of passengers were using the electronic gates because they were unfamiliar with them. We do not have current wait-time figures, so **do not plan tight connections on your arrival day** and keep your passport handy.

> **💡 Peru Beyond Tip:** If you have a connection to Cusco or a tour on the same day, leave at least three hours between landing in Lima and your next flight, to absorb immigration, baggage claim and customs.

## 5. Exit Ticket, Lodging and Proof of Funds

This point causes confusion because **none of the official sources we consulted lists it as a formal requirement**: Migraciones Perú does not mention it in its recommendations to foreign visitors. However, the **SRE traveler's guide** does advise carrying enough money to cover your stay and having ties that show your intention to return to Mexico.

In practice, keep the following in mind:

- **The airline decides at check-in:** many companies ask for a return or onward ticket to board for a visa-free country, and may deny boarding if you cannot show it.
- **The immigration officer may ask:** it is common to be asked about the purpose of your trip, your destinations and where you will stay.
- **Carry an organized backup:** return ticket, hotel booking or itinerary, and a card or reasonable amount of cash.

### 5.1. Documents worth carrying

## 6. Mexico City – Lima Flights: What to Expect

Routes between **Mexico City and Lima** take, according to flight search engines, roughly **six hours** on a nonstop flight. The airlines that fly nonstop change by season and by the source you check (search engines mention, among others, Aeroméxico, LATAM and Volaris), so the prudent thing is to **verify the current offer on each airline's website** before buying. Prices vary widely by date; rather than relying on a single figure, compare flexible dates.

### 6.1. Booking tips

- **Buy directly from an airline or a reputable agency** and keep the confirmation with your name exactly as it appears on your passport.
- **Arrive in Lima with a buffer:** if your next stop is Cusco, check domestic flight schedules and avoid arriving on an afternoon with very tight connections.
- **Decide whether you enter and exit via Lima:** if you are doing a circuit such as Lima, Paracas, Cusco and Machu Picchu, your return flight to Mexico will probably also leave from Lima.

## 7. Health and Vaccines: What Is Really Required

To enter Peru, **no vaccine is required** of travelers from Mexico, according to the official recommendations we reviewed. What does exist is a **health recommendation** from the Ministry of Health and its epidemiology directorate (DGE) about **yellow fever**:

- Vaccination is recommended for those entering **forested areas of the high or low jungle** in regions such as Amazonas, Ayacucho, Huánuco, Junín, Loreto, Madre de Dios, Pasco, Puno, San Martín, Ucayali and the Cusco jungle.
- The DGE clarifies that **the city of Cusco, Machu Picchu and the Inca Trail are excluded** from those risk zones.
- The vaccine should be given **at least 10 days before** travel.
- On some entries to jungle areas, a vaccination certificate may be requested.

To complete the picture, Peruvian and international media report that **neighboring countries, such as Ecuador, ask for a certificate from travelers arriving from Peru**, and that Costa Rica expanded its requirement in 2026. Those are the rules of other countries, not of Peru, so if you plan to continue your trip, check the requirements of your next destination.

### 7.1. Altitude and health in Cusco

Although there is no health procedure to complete, **Cusco sits at 3,399 m above sea level** and it is normal to feel altitude sickness during the first days. Bring personal medication with a prescription, stay hydrated, avoid alcohol on the first day and plan a gentle acclimatization day. **Travel insurance with international medical coverage**, recommended by the SRE, is especially valuable if you will go trekking.

## 8. Customs: Cash, Products and the Sworn Declaration

**SUNAT (the National Superintendency of Customs and Tax Administration)** regulates what you can bring into the country. These are the key points from its traveler guidance portal:

### 8.1. Cash

- **More than US$ 10,000** (or the equivalent in another currency): you must **declare it** on arrival.
- **More than US$ 30,000:** bringing in that amount in cash is not allowed; larger sums must go through the financial system.
- The declaration can be made at the airport or through the **Bienvenido al Perú** app, where the **digital Baggage Sworn Declaration** is also filed.

### 8.2. Products and baggage

- Goods that are not on the "nothing to declare" list and are not restricted are taxed: according to SUNAT, **up to US$ 1,000 pays 12%**, and above that the taxes of an import apply.
- **Restricted products** (they require authorization from one or more agencies) include agricultural products, medical and dental equipment, wild flora and fauna, weapons and ammunition, and cultural heritage items.
- Identifiable professional or technical equipment may enter under **temporary import**, with a guarantee.

> **💡 Peru Beyond Tip:** If you travel with a drone, a professional camera or costly work equipment, keep the invoices and consult SUNAT before the trip. Avoid bringing fruit, seeds, animal products or plants, as they may be seized.

## 9. Minors: Two Sides of the Process

If you travel with children, there is a key difference: Peru **does not require foreign minors who are tourists** to complete an entry form different from an adult's, but **Mexico does regulate the exit of minors**.

- **From Mexico:** according to the Ministry of the Interior (Segob) and the INM, Mexican minors leaving the country **alone, with only one parent or with a third party** must present the **exit authorization form (SAM)** or a notarized document from those holding parental authority. Consular sources indicate that the authorization is valid for a single exit and within 180 days of its registration. **Confirm the procedure, its validity and its current cost with the INM or your consulate.**
- **The minor's documents:** own valid passport and, if traveling with only one parent, a birth certificate proving the relationship.
- **About Peruvian travel authorization rules:** those described by Migraciones Perú (notarial, judicial or consular authorization) are aimed at Peruvian minors and foreign residents leaving the country, not at foreign tourists entering.

## 10. Pets: Traveling to Peru with Dogs and Cats

Entering Peru with a pet is possible, but it requires planning **months in advance**. **SENASA** (the National Agrarian Health Service), the Peruvian authority on the matter, states that the traveler must hold the **international health certificate issued by the competent authority of the country of origin** (in Mexico, the agricultural health authority) and pay the health inspection on arrival. SENASA has a digital platform, **"Viaja con tu mascota"**, with details of the procedures.

Because requirements for vaccines, microchip and certificate validity may vary, **do not buy tickets until you have confirmed the documentation with SENASA and the airline**, since many limit or prohibit pets in the cabin or hold depending on the route. And remember that most hotels and tourist trains to Machu Picchu have restrictions on animals.

## 14. Quick FAQs

**Can I enter with my INE voter ID or only with a passport?** For Mexicans, the travel document is the passport. Migraciones notes that citizens of Andean Community or Mercosur countries can enter with their national ID card, but Mexico is not on that list.

**What happens if I overstay?** Migraciones reports that overstaying is penalized with a daily fine equivalent to 0.1% of the UIT (Tax Unit), whose value changes each year; check the current amount before leaving.

**Where do I ask for help if I lose my passport?** At the nearest Mexican consulate, and request the safe-conduct. Registering with SIRME makes contact easier.

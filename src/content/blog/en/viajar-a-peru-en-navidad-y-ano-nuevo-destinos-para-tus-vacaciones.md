If you are wondering whether **traveling to Peru in December** is a good idea, the short answer is yes, with one important detail: in December Peru has three climates at once. The coast (Lima, Paracas, Ica) is in full summer, with sun and heat; the Andes (Cusco, Sacred Valley, Machu Picchu, Colca) enter the rainy season; and the Amazon is warm and humid. Travelers who plan with that in mind come home with an unforgettable holiday: green landscapes, unique Christmas traditions such as Cusco's Santurantikuy, and a New Year with rituals you will not find anywhere else.

In this **Peru Beyond** guide we cover the real weather destination by destination (with cited data), how Machu Picchu feels in the rainy season, what to do on Christmas Eve and New Year's Eve, how to handle prices and bookings in peak season, and we suggest 7 to 10 day itineraries so you can plan your holidays in Peru with confidence.

## 1. Peru's Weather in December: What to Expect at Each Destination

Peru does not have a single climate: the Andes and the coastal current split the country into very different regions. That is why, when **traveling to Peru in December**, you need to look at each destination separately. The table below summarizes the typical December weather based on published climate averages (see sources in the chart notes):

| Destination | Approx. altitude | Dec. high / low | Dec. rainfall | What to expect |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Lima (coast)** | 0 to 150 m | 23 °C / 18 °C | Practically none (0.3 mm) | Summer: clearer skies, beach atmosphere |
| **Paracas and Ica** | 0 to 410 m | Ica: 31 °C / 16 °C | Practically none (0.2 mm) | Sunny, dry desert, very hot at midday |
| **Cusco** | 3,399 m | 21 °C / 8 °C | High (about 125 mm) | Clear mornings, afternoon showers, cold nights |

## 2. Cusco and Machu Picchu in the Rainy Season

This is the number one question for anyone **traveling to Peru in December**: is Machu Picchu worth it when it rains? Yes, and here are the reasons and the precautions.

### 2.1. The Best of Going in December

- **Spectacular green scenery:** the terraces, mountains and Urubamba valley look their greenest, with clouds opening and closing over the citadel. It is one of the most photogenic times of year.
- **Smaller crowds than in the dry season,** although holiday dates concentrate visitors and quotas sell out.
- **Mornings with good visibility:** rain mostly falls in the afternoon and evening, so many mornings allow you to explore the citadel under partly clear skies.
- **Festive atmosphere in Cusco:** decorations, fairs and a calendar of unique traditions (see section 3).

### 2.2. What to Keep in Mind

- **Rain and mist:** they can hide the classic view of the citadel for a while. Patience is often rewarded when the clouds lift.
- **Possible logistical disruptions:** in the rainy season, landslides and mudslides have affected roads, railways and access points. This is uncommon in December but more frequent from January to March; having an agency that monitors conditions is a big advantage.
- **Slippery paths:** wet Inca stones call for shoes with good grip and trekking poles if you hike mountain routes.

### 2.3. Machu Picchu During the Holidays: Tickets, Quotas and the Inca Trail

- **Tickets:** the Machu Picchu citadel has a daily capacity regulated by the Ministry of Culture, with defined circuits and routes. For 2026, official media such as Andina and El Peruano reported maximum capacities of up to 5,600 visitors per day on specific holiday dates, including December 30 and 31 and January 1. Figures and sales methods change from year to year: always verify the rules in force for 2027 on the Ministry of Culture's official **Tu Boleto** portal.
- **Book early:** holiday-date quotas and the most convenient train times sell out first. Ideally, secure entrance tickets and trains several months ahead.
- **Inca Trail:** the Inca Trail network closes every February for maintenance. In 2026 the closure ran from January 31 until the end of February, reopening on March 1, and in 2025 it was extended to the end of March because of rain and landslides. No definitive notice for 2027 existed when this article was written, but a similar closure is reasonable to expect; if you dream of the Inca Trail in January, confirm dates with SERNANP and the Ministry of Culture.

> **💡 Peru Beyond Tip:** If your trip falls between late December and January, ask your agency for a plan B in case the train or road is interrupted: one extra buffer day at the end of the itinerary protects your international flight.

## 3. Christmas in Peru: Traditions Worth Experiencing

Peruvian Christmas blends Catholic heritage, Andean customs and Creole flavors. These are the traditions travelers enjoy most.

### 3.1. Santurantikuy: Cusco's Great Christmas Fair

**Santurantikuy** (Quechua for "saint selling") is a folk art fair held every December 24 in Cusco's Plaza de Armas. Artisans from several regions of the country arrive to sell religious figures, nativity scenes, **Niño Manuelito** statues and folk crafts. Press reports describe it as the largest folk art fair in Peru, with more than a thousand artisans in some editions; some accounts place its origins in the 19th century. Fairs usually begin a few days before the 24th; dates and hours can change every year, so confirm them with the Cusco municipality.

What to do there:

1. Walk the stalls early in the morning, when it is less crowded.
2. Buy a Niño Manuelito or a Cusco nativity scene as an authentic souvenir.
3. Try hot chocolate and sweet bread at the cafés on the plaza.
4. Watch your belongings: it is a very busy event.

### 3.2. Christmas Eve: Dinner, Toast and Panettone

In Peru, Christmas Eve is celebrated at midnight on December 24. Families gather for dinner, which typically includes turkey, salads, tamales and other dishes depending on the region. At 12 sharp there is a toast, everyone hugs and gifts are opened; fireworks complete the scene in many neighborhoods.

- **Panettone and chocolatada:** panettone (sweet bread with candied fruit) with hot chocolate is the culinary symbol of Peruvian Christmas. **Chocolatadas** are gatherings, often charitable, where hot chocolate and panettone are handed out, especially to children, in neighborhoods, schools and plazas.
- **Midnight Mass:** many parishes hold a midnight service. The cathedrals and churches of Cusco, Lima and Arequipa are especially decorated.
- **Andean nativity scenes:** in Cusco nativity scenes mix colonial figures, the Niño Manuelito and local elements such as llamas and Andean landscapes.

> **💡 Peru Beyond Tip:** On the evening of the 24th, many restaurants close early or only offer a special menu by reservation. If you prefer to dine out, book weeks ahead or ask your hotel for a Christmas Eve dinner.

### 3.3. What to Expect on December 25

December 25 is a national holiday and most Peruvians rest with family after the night before. Many shops open late or close, and public transport runs on reduced schedules. For travelers it is a great day for activities already arranged, such as a Sacred Valley excursion, or simply for a relaxed stroll. Always confirm opening hours of museums and attractions in advance, as they can change on holidays.

## 5. Destinations for Your Year-End Holidays

### 5.1. Lima: Your Starting Point in the Limeño Summer

In December Lima is in full summer: clearer skies than in winter, highs around 23 °C (73 °F) and a beach atmosphere. It is ideal for strolling the Miraflores boardwalk, the bohemian district of Barranco and the Historic Center decked out for Christmas, plus sampling the best food in South America. Book restaurants ahead for the 24th and 31st.

### 5.2. Paracas and Ica: Desert, Sea and Sun

The southern coast is December's big winner: almost no rain and plenty of sun. Paracas offers its National Reserve, the Ballestas Islands and desert landscapes facing the ocean; Ica adds the Huacachina oasis with dune buggy rides and sandboarding. With highs near 31 °C (88 °F) in Ica, bring sunscreen, a hat and plenty of water.

### 5.3. Cusco and the Sacred Valley: Andean Tradition at the Holidays

At 3,399 m (11,152 ft), Cusco demands acclimatization. Spend your first day resting, drinking water and eating light. After that you can explore the historic center, Sacsayhuamán, Qoricancha and the Sacred Valley towns of Pisac, Ollantaytambo and Urubamba. Mornings are the best hours for outdoor excursions; afternoons are for cafés, museums and markets. If you want a short circuit, look at our 4-day Cusco, Sacred Valley and Machu Picchu tour.

### 5.4. Arequipa and the Colca Canyon: High-Altitude Sun and Volcanoes

Arequipa, at 2,335 m (7,661 ft), has a relatively dry December in the city (about 4 mm on average), with high-altitude sun and cool nights. Its white sillar historic center, the Santa Catalina Monastery and Arequipeña cuisine make it a great addition to extend your itinerary. In the Colca Canyon summer rains intensify from January to March, so in December it is wise to check conditions before booking the excursion.

### 5.5. The Amazon: Jungle at Its Peak

The Peruvian Amazon in December is warm and very rainy (in Puerto Maldonado the average tops 270 mm). In return it offers lush jungle, full rivers and active wildlife. If it appeals to you, plan with an agency and with lodges suited to the rainy season, keeping in mind that some river transfers can be delayed. If you are traveling deep into the jungle, ask about the yellow fever vaccine and other health recommendations from Peru's Ministry of Health at least 10 days ahead.

## 8. What to Pack for a Holiday Trip to Peru

- **Warm layers:** Cusco can drop to a low of about 8 °C (46 °F) in December; combine fleece, a light jacket and thermal wear.
- **Rain poncho or jacket** and closed shoes with good grip for Machu Picchu and trails.
- **Sunscreen, hat and sunglasses:** the high-altitude sun and the coastal sun are both intense.
- **Light clothing** for Lima, Paracas and Ica, plus a swimsuit if your hotel has a pool.
- **Yellow underwear**, in case you want to join the New Year ritual.
- **Documents and travel insurance** with international medical coverage, plus copies of your ticket and train bookings.
- **Insect repellent** if you include the jungle or Machupicchu Pueblo.

## 9. Common Mistakes When Traveling to Peru at Christmas and New Year

- **Booking late:** the most expensive mistake; Machu Picchu quotas and the best hotels sell out.
- **Ignoring altitude:** going up to Cusco without acclimatization ruins the first days.
- **Tight flights on the 31st and 1st:** always leave a buffer between flights and excursions.
- **Expecting clear skies all day in the Andes:** bring a plan B for afternoons.
- **Forgetting that the 25th and 1st are holidays:** some services run reduced hours.
